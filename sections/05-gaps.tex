\section{Gaps and Research Questions}
\label{sec:gaps}
The review shows that GPUs can run a radar chain, but the literature fails to say which resource bounds each stage, or where the GPU stops being fast. Three gaps stand out, and each is open on current hardware.

\paragraph{G1: Workload characterisation}
The intensities in Table~\ref{tab:workload} are first-order estimates.
Only Venter has measured them across a chain, on a 2008-era GPU with one channel \cite{venterSOFTWAREDEFINEDPULSEDOPPLERRADAR},
and no study compares platforms on the same chain (Section~\ref{sec:reporting}).
\emph{RQ1: Which resource (host-link, device-memory bandwidth, launch overhead, on-chip capacity, or compute) bounds each stage of a multi-channel pulse-Doppler and STAP chain on current GPUs, and how does this change with CPI size and platform?}

\paragraph{G2: Ingest and memory model}
Transfers dominate where they are measured, a modest CPI already exceeds what PCIe~4.0 sustains,
and the alternatives are untested for radar (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
\emph{RQ2: What share of the CPI budget does data ingest take under pageable, pinned, unified, integrated, and direct (GPUDirect) memory, and at what CPI size does the chain become link-bound?}

\paragraph{G3: Execution model}
Streams give modest, confounded gains, no study uses CUDA Graphs or persistent kernels,
and overlap can raise throughput while nearly doubling latency \cite{venterSOFTWAREDEFINEDPULSEDOPPLERRADAR},
yet only one study reports timing spread (Sections~\ref{sec:reporting} and~\ref{sec:execution}).
\emph{RQ3: Which of sequential launches, streams, CUDA Graphs or persistent kernels
  gives the lowest tail latency at a given throughput for a repeating, fixed-shape CPI pipeline?}

G1 sets the baseline that G2 and G3 vary,
so the three questions form a single study on one chain.
To avoid the weaknesses of Section~\ref{sec:reporting},
it should use an optimised multithreaded CPU baseline, time transfers explicitly,
and report latency distributions rather than means, following the criteria of Table~\ref{tab:rubric}.

Two further gaps are narrower.
\textbf{G4:} implementations that adapt to parameters and hardware remain Benson et al.'s open problem \cite{bensonGPUbasedSpacetimeAdaptive2013a},
although auto-tuning has addressed it for a memory-bound kernel in radio astronomy \cite{scloccoRealtimeDedispersionFast2016a}.
\textbf{G5:} whether 16-bit or integer arithmetic on tensor cores preserves radar detection performance is untested (Section~\ref{sec:precision}).
Finally, deploying such chains in airborne systems raises timing-assurance and certification questions, such as DO-178C,
that this review does not address;
Trompouki and Kosmidis review approaches to certifying GPU software \cite{mariatrompoukiDO178CCertificationGeneralPurpose2021a}.
