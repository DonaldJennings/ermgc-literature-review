\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Search Strategy}
The corpus of studies was built in two stages. First, papers already held were used as seeds \cite{zhaoGPUAcceleratedSignalProcessing2023, bensonGPUbasedSpacetimeAdaptive2013a, perdanaSurveyGraphicsProcessing2017}. The reference lists for these papers were followed backwards,
citing and same-author work was followed forwards. These were supplemented by targeted searched on the themes raised in Section~\ref{sec:intro} on data movement, execution models, auto-tuning, reduced precision and timing measurements. Adjacent domains with mature GPU
streaming practice, namely radio astronomy were included where they hold the strongest evidence for operations radar shares. Secondly,
to make the search reproducible, two strings were run in IEEE Xplore and the ACM Digital Library
\begin{itemize}
  \item (GPU OR CUDA OR GPGPU) AND (radar or ``synthetic aperture'' OR STAP OR CFAR OR ``pulse compression'' OR beamforming)
  \item (GPU OR CUDA) AND (roofline OR benchmarking OR ``unified memory'' OR ``CUDA Graphs'' OR auto-tuning)
\end{itemize}

These returned N1 and N2 records respectively, of which N3 were added to this study. Recent preprints were drawn from arXiv and are labelled for highlighting.

\subsection{Selection}
Studies were included if they (i)~implement or measure a radar or sensor signal processing stage on a GPU, or supply a high-performance computing method used to judge such work; (ii)~report quantitative performance and (iii)~ are peer-reviewed or , in the case of a thesis or technical report, contain a clear methodology. Issues surrounding certification, fault tolerance, real-time scheduling theory and performance portability were excluded as out of scope. A total of 73 items were identified, 35 of which are included in this review, spannig 1995 to 2026, with 21 published in 2016 or later.

\subsection{Appraisal}
Each of the 15 implementation studies was read in full and recorded in an
evidence matrix against six criteria (Table~\ref{tab:rubric}).
Three follow Hoefler and Belli's rules for interpretable benchmarking: the CPU
baseline, since a speed-up means little without the absolute performance of
its base case; the depth of analysis, since bounds such as the roofline make a
result interpretable; and the timing statistics, since nondeterministic
measurements need a measure of spread
\cite{hoeflerScientificBenchmarkingParallel2015a}.
Whether transfers are timed follows Gregg and Hazelwood
\cite{greggWhereDataWhy2011a}.
The execution model and energy measurement were added from the review question
itself: the first is an implementation strategy, and the second a cost that is
rarely measured directly.
Cells record only what each paper states; anything unstated was recorded as
unclear rather than inferred.
The review had a single reviewer, so screening and appraisal were not
independently checked; verifying every matrix row against the full text
partly mitigates this.
